Let a fast AI take the easy orthopaedic questions, and save the big AI for the hard ones.

This is a proof of concept. Can System One models decide which orthopaedic exam questions need an expensive reasoning model, and which do not?

System One models, such as Jev from TypeSafe, Liquid d1-ultra and Liquid d1.1 from Liquid AI, are a new kind of AI. TypeSafe released the first one, Jev, in September 2026, and raised an $870 million Series A led by Andreessen Horowitz in October 2026. Instead of writing out its reasoning, a System One model reads a question and returns an answer straight away, along with how sure it is. That makes it very fast and very cheap, but on its own it is less accurate than large models such as Claude Opus.

System One models such as Jev score lower than Opus on their own, but Jev knows when it is right. Letting Jev answer first and sending only the unsure questions to Claude Opus matched Opus alone, 12× faster and 1.7× cheaper.

But Jev knows when it is right.

When Jev was 95% sure of its answer, it was right 98.5% of the time. That happened on 1,491 of 3,019 questions.

Combine the two and you match the big model.

Jev answers first. Only the questions it is unsure about go to Claude Opus. Jev+Claude Opus scores the same as Opus alone, 12× faster and 1.7× cheaper.

Real time. Every question goes to Jev first, which takes 0.21 s. If Jev is 95% sure or more, its answer stands. If not, the question goes on to Claude Opus, which takes 3.88 s more. Here 10 of 20 questions go to Opus. In the real test, 49% did.

Limits

  • Source. MedMCQA questions come from Indian medical entrance exams, not US boards or real patient cases.
  • Format. Multiple choice and text only. A few questions refer to images the models did not see.
  • Sample size. The big models answered a random 500 of the 3,019 questions.
